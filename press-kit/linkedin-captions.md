## 📚 LONG (4-5 paragraphs, narrative) — ~290 words

> Showcasing one of my favorite SEIS 666 final projects this semester
> at @University of St. Thomas — **Wearly**, built by **Eatedal Alfadhel**.
>
> *Wearly is an evidence-informed, body-positive personal styling
> agent.* Not a chatbot, an *agent*: context-first stance, seven-step
> reasoning loop, visible reasoning trail, reject-and-regenerate,
> stateful across sessions. Every recommendation cites a named rule
> a reviewer can audit.
>
> What I think makes this stand out from a typical course project:
>
> **1. End-to-end and shippable.** A live Streamlit app, a Level-2
> Intelligent Textbook (per Dan McCreary's framework), and a structured
> Knowledge Graph — all deployed, all interconnected, 393 tests passing.
>
> **2. Evidence-informed.** Eight rule packs documented as a Claude
> Skill, each citing Vasileva 2018, Han 2017, Itten's color theory, the
> McGuire body-shape framework, and GDPR-aligned privacy guidance.
> Citations live in code (`rule_refs.py`) and print on every reasoning
> line.
>
> **3. Body-positive by design.** Wearly's language contract is
> enforced at the prose layer by an integration test — not as a
> stylistic guideline. Fit advice is reframed from "flaws to hide" to
> "silhouette emphasis." Deliberate, auditable, testable.
>
> **4. Structured knowledge for the LLM era.** The Wearly Knowledge
> Graph (91 nodes / 187 typed edges across three layers — domain rules,
> user behavior, runtime archetypes) is the substrate a future RAG or
> LLM shopping agent can traverse along named edges rather than
> vector-searching free text. The architecture is ready for the layers
> that will sit on top of it.
